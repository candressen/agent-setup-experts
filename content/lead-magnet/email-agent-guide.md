# Page 2: The Problem

## Email Is Quietly Eating Your Workday

For many small business owners, email is not just a communication tool anymore. It has become a full-time distraction. Between customer questions, sales inquiries, vendor updates, newsletters, internal threads, and spam, your inbox can pull you away from the work that actually grows the business.

Many owners spend **4+ hours per day** in email alone. That is half a workday lost to sorting, replying, forwarding, checking in, and trying not to miss anything important.

### The real cost of inbox overload:
- **Missed leads** because promising emails get buried
- **Delayed responses** that make your business look slower than it is
- **Mental drain** from context-switching all day long
- **Decision fatigue** from repeatedly asking, “Do I need to handle this now?”

### Common pain points business owners face:
- Constant **spam and low-value messages**
- Endless **CC chains** with no clear next step
- Writing the same **repetitive replies** over and over
- No reliable system for **triaging urgent vs. non-urgent emails**

The problem is not that you are bad at email. The problem is that your inbox has never had a smart system working for you.

# Page 3: What an Email Manager Agent Does

## Your First Line of Defense for Inbox Control

An email manager AI agent acts like a digital inbox assistant. Its job is to review incoming messages, apply your rules, and help you respond faster without forcing you to touch every email yourself.

### A simple email manager agent can:
- **Sort your inbox automatically** into categories like:
  - **Action Required**
  - **FYI**
  - **Junk**
- **Auto-draft replies** to common messages such as scheduling requests, pricing questions, intake forms, and routine customer support
- **Flag urgent emails** so the most important items rise to the top
- **Send follow-ups** for messages that have gone unanswered too long
- **Block spam at the source** by identifying repeat junk patterns and filtering them out

Instead of opening every email one by one, you review a cleaner inbox with priorities already surfaced. That means less busywork, faster replies, and fewer dropped balls.

# Page 4: What You Need to Build It

## The Basic Ingredients

You do not need a massive software stack to build a simple version of this.

### Here is what you need:
- **An email account**  
  Gmail is the easiest place to start because it connects well with automation tools.

- **A free or paid AI tool**  
  Good beginner options include:
  - **ChatGPT** for writing logic and drafting response templates
  - **Claude** for creating cleaner classification rules and summaries
  - **Zapier + Gmail** for connecting triggers, labels, drafts, and actions

- **2 to 3 hours of setup time**  
  Enough to define your email categories, build a first workflow, and test it.

- **A list of your most common email types**  
  For example:
  - New leads
  - Customer support questions
  - Appointment requests
  - Vendor messages
  - Newsletters or promotions

The key is not building something perfect on day one. The goal is to create a first system that saves you time immediately.

# Page 5: Step-by-Step Setup

## A Simple DIY Workflow

### Step 1: Audit your inbox
Look through the last 50 to 100 emails in your inbox and identify your **top 5 categories**. Keep it simple. Most business inboxes can be grouped into a few predictable buckets.

Example categories:
- Lead or sales inquiry
- Existing customer request
- Internal team update
- Newsletter or promotion
- Spam or irrelevant

### Step 2: Write your sorting rules in plain English
Before touching any automation tool, write out the logic the way you would explain it to an assistant.

Example:
- “If the email contains words like quote, pricing, estimate, or proposal, label it **Action Required: Lead**.”
- “If it is from an existing customer, label it **Action Required: Client**.”
- “If it is a newsletter, mark it **FYI**.”
- “If it looks promotional or suspicious, move it to **Junk**.”

This step matters because AI performs best when your instructions are clear and specific.

### Step 3: Connect your AI tool to Gmail
A beginner-friendly setup is **Zapier + Gmail**.

Basic walkthrough:
1. Create a Zap with **New Email in Gmail** as the trigger
2. Send the email content to your AI step for classification
3. Return a category such as **Action Required**, **FYI**, or **Junk**
4. Use Gmail actions to:
   - Apply a label
   - Star urgent emails
   - Create a draft reply if needed
   - Move junk messages out of your main inbox

If you want to go one level deeper, you can also add response templates for common email types.

### Step 4: Test with 10 emails
Do not turn it loose on your full inbox immediately. Start with 10 recent emails and review every result.

Check:
- Did it sort the message correctly?
- Did it flag anything urgent?
- Did any good emails get treated like junk?
- Were the draft replies accurate and on-brand?

### Step 5: Refine and automate
After testing, improve your rules based on what went wrong.

You may need to:
- Add better keyword examples
- Create more specific categories
- Exclude certain senders
- Tighten your spam logic
- Improve the tone of your draft replies

Once it is working well, let the system run automatically and review it daily instead of manually processing everything yourself.
