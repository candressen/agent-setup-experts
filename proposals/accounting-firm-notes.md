# Accounting Firm Proposal Notes

## 5 Talking Points for Christian
1. This is not about replacing CPAs. It is about removing repetitive admin so the team can spend more time on review, advisory work, and client service.
2. The biggest immediate win is document collection. If ASE shortens the back-and-forth by even a few days per client, it directly improves turnaround and staff capacity during tax season.
3. Bookkeeping automation is recommendation-based, not auto-posting. The AI does first-pass extraction and categorization, but the firm keeps final approval on every entry.
4. Security is a design requirement, not an afterthought. Lead with IRS Publication 4557 alignment, human checkpoints, role-based access, masked PII, and no training on client data.
5. The rollout is intentionally phased. Start with one workflow, prove value quickly, then expand into additional operations once the team is comfortable.

## 3 Likely Objections and How to Handle Them

### 1. "We cannot risk client tax data with AI."
**How to handle it:**
Acknowledge that concern directly and agree with it. Explain that the system is structured around taxpayer data safeguards, not open-ended AI use. Emphasize US-based infrastructure, encryption in transit and at rest, no model training on client data, role-based access, audit logs, masked identifiers, and human approval before any sensitive action.

### 2. "Our team already has a process, even if it is manual."
**How to handle it:**
Position ASE as a workflow layer, not a forced replacement of everything they do today. The goal is to keep what works and remove the repetitive bottlenecks, especially reminders, intake tracking, status communication, and first-pass data extraction. Offer to start with one narrow use case rather than changing the whole firm at once.

### 3. "AI makes mistakes, so we do not want automated accounting or tax prep."
**How to handle it:**
Agree with the premise that unsupervised automation would be risky. Then clarify that this proposal is not autonomous filing or auto-posting. It is AI-assisted preparation with explicit CPA review checkpoints. Frame it as a productivity tool that prepares work for professionals, not a substitute for professional judgment.

## Questions to Ask the Prospect to Qualify Them
- What part of the workflow creates the most friction today: document collection, client communication, bookkeeping cleanup, or tax data entry?
- During busy season, where does your team lose the most non-billable time each week?
- How are clients currently submitting documents, and how do you track what is still missing?
- Which bookkeeping platforms and tax software does your team use today?
- Do you want to start with tax workflow improvements, bookkeeping workflow improvements, or both?
- Who on your team would need approval visibility: admin staff, preparers, reviewers, partners?
- What security or compliance requirements are non-negotiable for your firm?
- Have you already documented a Written Information Security Plan, or would you want the solution mapped to your existing controls?
- How many clients or monthly bookkeeping accounts would you want included in an initial rollout?
- If this works well, what outcome would matter most to you: faster turnaround, fewer interruptions, better consistency, or more capacity without additional hires?
